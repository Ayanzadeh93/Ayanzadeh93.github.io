ADHD Study Pack

A Pomodoro Workspace Built Around How a Study Session Actually Falls Apart

Aydin Ayanzadeh
Runs in the browser · optional Google sync
2026
Open the Study Pack All Projects

Overview

Most study tools assume the hard part is knowing what to do. For an ADHD working session it usually is not — the hard part is starting, staying started, and telling what is loud apart from what matters.

The Study Pack is a Pomodoro workspace built for that. The focus dial runs whatever interval is survivable today, down to a two-minute start, and asks one sentence about what “done” looks like before it begins. An Eisenhower matrix makes the urgent-versus-important call once, in advance, instead of forty times a day under pressure. The planner turns “I should study” into blocks with a start time, and a distracting thought is one keystroke from parked rather than a new tab.

Nothing is pre-filled. Every chart, streak and suggestion is computed from what you actually do, so the statistics are evidence about when you focus well and what pulls you off — not a scoreboard.

What It Does

Focus dial. Adjustable focus, short-break and long-break lengths with presets from “Two-minute start” to 90/20 ultradian blocks. The countdown tracks the wall clock, so a backgrounded tab cannot run it slow. A session intent, an activation rating, a one-tap distraction tally and a quiet body-double mode ride alongside.

Priority matrix. Tasks sit in Q1–Q4 by urgency and importance, placed by drag-and-drop or two taps, with a one-at-a-time sorter for the unsorted pile. A balance card shows how much focus time really went to Q2, and the focus view nudges toward a Q2 task when the week is running on urgency.

Tasks and planner. Every task carries a pomodoro estimate and an activation cost — how hard it is to start. Any task or calendar block can also be timed with a stopwatch: press start, work for as long as it runs, and the minutes land in your study time. A week calendar holds classes and study blocks; courses get weekly targets, and the auto-scheduler packs sessions into free hours, weighted by the hours you historically focus best. Connect Google Calendar and the sync runs both ways: your Google events appear in the planner as busy time, and the blocks you plan are added to Google, with edits and deletions following them. Calendars also come in and go out as .ics.

Notes, sound and calm. A sticky-note wall where parked thoughts land; rain, brown and pink noise, café hum, fireplace and more, all synthesised live with the Web Audio API; breathing pacers, a 5-4-3-2-1 grounding run, check-ins and movement prompts for the break.

Statistics. Focus minutes per day, best hours, a consistency heatmap, what pulls you away, time by course, a session log with CSV export, and plain-language insights drawn from all of it.

Where the Data Lives

Two ways in: sign in with Google and the workspace syncs, or use a local profile and nothing leaves the browser.

Google sign-in keeps the workspace in Cloud Firestore at users/{uid}/workspace/state. Firestore security rules let only the signed-in owner read or write it. Open tabs and devices update live, and the page keeps working offline, queueing writes until the connection is back. If the synced workspace cannot be loaded, the app signs back out rather than opening an empty one that could overwrite it.

A local profile lives in this browser only. Its password is hashed before it is stored, but the page says plainly what that is: a courtesy lock on a shared laptop, not security. Either way, the whole workspace exports as JSON and imports back in one step.

Design & Accessibility

Every view and every timer action has a single-key shortcut, and a command palette (Ctrl/⌘ + K) reaches the rest, so the mouse is never the thing standing between you and starting. B parks a thought and D logs a distraction without stopping the timer.

Light and dark follow the system or can be pinned, with four accent colours. Under prefers-reduced-motion the breathing and pulse animations stop. On a phone the navigation becomes a scrolling strip and every view stacks to a single column.

Technologies Used

Vanilla JavaScript ES Modules Web Audio API SVG Charts Firebase Authentication Cloud Firestore CSS Custom Properties

No framework, no bundler and no build step — the browser runs the source exactly as committed. The Firebase SDK is loaded only when the page is configured for it, from a pinned version, and the page’s Content Security Policy allows no inline script.
