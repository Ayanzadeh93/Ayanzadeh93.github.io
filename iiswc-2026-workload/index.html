Characterizing an On-Device Depth-Aware Multimodal Workload in Assistive Navigation for Blind and Low-Vision Users

  • Aydin Ayanzadeh
  • Tim Oates

University of Maryland, Baltimore County

Paper Code Slides

Abstract

Assistive visual navigation for blind and low-vision users is shifting from geometry-only pipelines to detector-plus-small-multimodal-LLM (sMLLM) pipelines that deliver semantically grounded guidance. The resulting workload, however, has not been systematically characterized: prior studies emphasize API latency or natural-language quality and ignore on-device systems behaviour. We present the first end-to-end workload characterization of a two-stage YOLO + sMLLM pipeline operating under a hard 300 ms per-frame safety service-level objective (SLO). We expose all five execution phases—detector inference, vision-encoder embedding, MLLM prefill, autoregressive decode, and KV-cache materialization—and introduce a thirteen-group metric taxonomy that unifies compute, memory hierarchy, interconnect, energy, latency tails, throughput, LLM-phase, visual-token, scheduling, thermal/DVFS, SLO, quality, and robustness. The taxonomy is instantiated through NVML, DCGM, Nsight Systems/Compute, RAPL, pyJoules, and Linux perf. Across four detector × sMLLM configurations and three urgency-stratified scene suites, we identify two principal bottlenecks: decode-bound tail latency that produces an order-of-magnitude gap between mean and p99, and visual-token inflation that drives prefill cost super-linearly with input resolution. We further show that detector-side optimization is a low-leverage knob (4–6% of frame energy), whereas output-token capping, INT8 quantization, and visual-token pruning recover up to 27% of safety goodput. We release a per-frame CSV schema, instrumentation hooks, and figure-regenerating scripts as a reproducible IISWC artifact.
